    Individual Block Diagram

    Overview

    To understand the light sensor subsystem, I have included my individual block diagram, which is available in several formats for your review. My subsystem features a photoresistor, which will be connected to a unity gain buffer to condition the signal, and then to an inverting amplifier, which will amplify and invert the signal for further processing. This will produce an amplified analog output, which can then be processed by the microcontroller and tested to determine if the light levels are too low. When the light level is too low, the microcontroller sends a digital high signal to the speaker subsystem, alerting the user.

    There are two power levels: a 9V power level supplied by the barrel jack adapter and a 5V power level supplied by the voltage regulator component. The unregulated 9V level is regulated by the 5V voltage regulator, which then powers the op-amp and photoresistor, creating my analog signal. The listed pins are used to process signals, as RA0 is an analog-capable pin that can utilize the ADC system to allow for signal conditioning. The other pins, RD7, RC2, and RC3, are simple digital I/O pins for controlling digital high or low signals.

    This design meets the product requirements by being easily customizable through signal processing, allowing for changes to the light thresholds of the sensor. This design is also simple enough to be compact and easy to integrate into the overall system for ease of use. This design, of course, is specifically for the prototype made for this class, but it could easily be adapted into a real product, as it only requires a few components and connections.
